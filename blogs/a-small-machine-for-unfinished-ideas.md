Most ideas arrive without a product brief. They show up as a sharp edge in an ordinary day: a command that takes three steps too many, a page that almost feels right, a question that refuses to close.

I have learned to give those ideas a small machine.

## Start with a narrow door

The first version should do one memorable thing. Not *everything the idea might become* — just the part that makes you want to come back tomorrow.

```ts
const nextQuestion = await makeTheSmallestUsefulThing()
console.log(nextQuestion)
```

That constraint is less about discipline than it is about listening. A small surface makes the signal louder. You can feel where the idea has energy, and where you are only decorating it.

> Build the smallest thing that can disagree with you.

## Leave the seams visible

I like tools that show their joins: the raw data, the awkward state transition, the little note explaining why a strange decision exists. Those seams are not unfinished business. They are invitations for the next question.

The goal is not to turn every experiment into a product. Sometimes the useful result is simply a better vocabulary for the next attempt.

## Keep the loop open

When a prototype survives a few weeks of real use, it earns a second room: tests, sharper edges, a name. Until then, I keep it close, small, and easy to change.

That is the kind of work I want to make more often — software with enough structure to be useful, and enough pulse to remain curious.
